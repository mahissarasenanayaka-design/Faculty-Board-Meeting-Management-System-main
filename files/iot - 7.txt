Introduction to IoT Security and
Privacy

¢ The Internet of Things (IoT) connects billions of devices like smartwatches,
home security systems, and industrial machines to the internet.

« These devices collect, process, and share data to make life easier and

improve efficiency.

« However, as IoT grows, so do the risks associated with its use.
What is IoT Security?
¢IoT security refers to the measures and practices used to protect IoT
devices, networks, and the data they generate from unauthorized access,
theft, or damage.

« Key Objective: Ensure the confidentiality, integrity, and availability of IoT

systems.

¢ Why Important? IoT devices often operate in critical areas like healthcare,
transportation, and homes, where a security breach can cause significant

harm.
What is IoT Privacy?
Internet of Things Applications

«IoT privacy focuses on protecting personal and sensitive information
collected by IoT devices. It ensures that users have control over how their
data is collected, stored, and shared.

« Key Objective: Maintain user trust by safeguarding their data.

¢ Why Important? Many IoT devices collect highly personal information, such
as health stats, location, or daily habits.
Why IoT Security and Privacy Are
Critical

« Increased Connectivity

¢ With billions of devices connected, a single vulnerability can affect multiple

systems.

« Example: A weakly secured smart bulb can become an entry point for
hackers to access a home network.
Internet of Things Applications

- Diverse Devices and Standards

¢eIoT devices come from various manufacturers with different security

standards, creating inconsistencies.

« Example: Some devices might not support encryption, making them easier

targets.
Internet of Things Applications

- Sensitive Data

¢IoT collects a large amount of data, often about personal or business

activities.

« Example: Fitness trackers log health metrics, which can be exploited if
leaked.
Internet of Things Applications

¢ Critical Infrastructure Dependency

¢IoT is integral to essential services like energy grids, water supply, and
healthcare.

« Example: A hacked smart grid could cause power outages in a city.
Challenges in IoT Security and
Privacy

¢ Limited Device Resources

« Many IoT devices are small and lack the processing power for advanced
security features like encryption.

« Example: A cheap smart sensor might not support regular updates or

complex security protocols.
Internet of Things Applications

« Lack of User Awareness

« Users often do not understand the security risks of IoT devices, leading to
weak passwords or unprotected networks.

« Example: A user may connect their smart TV to a public Wi-Fi network
without knowing the risks.
